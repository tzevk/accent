# Lead work is one activity log, and the Lead-to-Proposal handoff is one transaction

Accent had Leads, per-Lead follow-up rows, and a Lead-to-Proposal handoff, and none of them agreed. The follow-up rows carried a free-text type, a status string, and no author, no call outcome, and no occurrence time. The stage labels disagreed between the create form, the edit form, the import, and the report counters. The handoff was two client requests: one that created a Proposal, another that updated the Lead. Two users could therefore create two Proposals for one enquiry, and a failure in the middle left a Lead that said "Converted" and a Proposal nobody linked.

We decided that the Lead work is one log, that the stage vocabulary is one list, and that the handoff is one server action:

- **One log.** The legacy `follow_ups` table becomes `lead_activities`. A row is either scheduled work (`status = 'Scheduled'`) or recorded evidence (`status = 'Completed'` or `'Cancelled'`). The kind of work, the call outcome, and the task status are three different columns, because they answer three different questions: what kind of work, what happened during the call, and whether the work is still to come. A future task needs no outcome; a finished attempt can be "No Answer" and still be a completed call.
- **One vocabulary.** New, Attempting Contact, Connected, Qualified, Nurture, Disqualified, and Converted, in one client-safe module that the server, the interface, the import, and the counters share. Proposal negotiation, Proposal approval, and a won sale are not Lead Stages.
- **One accountable owner.** `leads.owner_user_id` is the authority; the old display name stays as evidence. Ownership is not an editing restriction: other permitted users may record work, and the Lead keeps one accountable owner.
- **The handoff is one transaction.** The Proposal, the Lead's converted stage, the conversion evidence, and the transfer of the scheduled work are written together. A failure rolls the whole handoff back: no converted Lead without its Proposal, no orphaned Proposal, no half-transferred work, no conversion history for a handoff that did not happen.
- **One canonical Proposal per enquiry.** A unique index over the canonical linkage makes a second authoritative Proposal impossible in the database, and the Lead row is locked while the handoff runs, so two concurrent requests converge on the same Proposal. A repeated request returns that Proposal and writes nothing.
- **The work moves, the evidence stays.** The completed activities of a Lead stay on the Lead after the handoff. The scheduled work moves to the Proposal with a traceable link in both directions, leaves the Lead queues, and is counted once.

The stage moves forward on its own in exactly two cases: the first outreach moves New to Attempting Contact, and a connected call or a confirmed email reply moves New or Attempting Contact to Connected. A note, a scheduled task, and a sent email nobody answered never prove contact. Nothing moves a Lead out of Qualified, Nurture, Disqualified, or Converted, and an automatic update never overwrites an explicit decision. Qualifying, nurturing, disqualifying, and reopening are accountable actions with a reason, an actor, and a time; they are never inferred from an activity.

Corrections keep the truth of the history. A correction writes the previous values, the reason, the correcting user from the session, and the time into an append-only table, and updates the activity's current values. The corrected activity stays one activity in every total, and a stale correction is refused instead of overwriting somebody else's accepted correction. Nothing is deleted: there is no delete for a Lead activity, and cancelling scheduled work keeps the row as evidence.

Considered: (a) keep the follow-up rows and add the missing columns to them — rejected, a table named after one kind of work cannot hold the other kinds, and the two would drift; (b) keep a separate activities table and a separate tasks table — rejected, one work item would then live in two places, and completing a task would have to delete one row and insert another, losing the link; (c) let the client create the Proposal and then update the Lead — rejected, two requests cannot be one transaction, and nothing prevents the second from running alone; (d) choose the canonical Proposal by name or by date when several are linked — rejected, recency is not evidence of authority, and an unresolved link is a review conflict that the handoff must refuse; (e) let a generic Proposal create or edit set the Lead link — rejected, that path would write a second authoritative link around the canonical one; (f) let the activity log advance the stage on any recorded work — rejected, then a note would qualify an enquiry and a failed call would qualify a client.

Consequences: the Lead detail gains one chronological history — activities, scheduled work, stage changes, owner changes, corrections, and the conversion — and a summary that separates the last successful contact from the latest attempt, so a failed call is never presented as contact. The Lead workspace gains the queues that this log makes possible: My Leads, Unassigned, Due Today, Overdue, Untouched, and No Next Action, with counters over the whole filtered dataset. The legacy rows keep their dates and their unknown facts: the old records never stored who acted, what a call achieved, or when a stage was entered, and the interface says so instead of guessing. A converted Lead takes no new Lead-side sales work; the work continues on its Proposal, and the Lead keeps showing that Proposal's progress.
