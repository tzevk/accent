# Project Expenditure Accounting — Research Note

**Status:** management-reporting research note, 2026-10-06. Not an accounting-policy decision (ADR/glossary owned elsewhere) and not a certification of statutory compliance. It records what official guidance supports, so the monthly report can use defensible definitions, and it states where evidence is still required.

**How sources are cited:** full IFRS texts are subscription-gated on ifrs.org, so paragraph-level proof below is read from the free official **ICAI Ind AS texts** (Companies Act, 2013 s.133; ICAI compendium), whose paragraph numbering follows the corresponding IAS/IFRS standards for the material used here. IFRS Foundation overview pages are linked for context only. No paywalled text is claimed as read.

---

## 1. What this report is, and is not

- The project expenditure report is a **management report**, presented outside the financial statements. Ind AS 1.13–14 (IAS 1.13–14) describes management reviews presented outside the statements, including resources not recognised in the balance sheet, and states such reports are **outside the scope of Ind AS**.
- The revised **IFRS Practice Statement 1 _Management Commentary_** (IASB, June 2025) is the official benchmark for this kind of narrative: management's perspective, material information, connected fact-based narrative, six content areas (business model, strategy, resources and relationships, external environment, risks, financial performance and position). The Practice Statement explicitly says application **is not required to comply with IFRS Standards**.
- Statutory anchors it must not overstate: Companies Act, 2013 **s.128(1)** requires books of account on the **accrual basis** and double-entry, giving a true and fair view. This report is not those books, and publishing it is not a compliance statement.
- Practical consequence: **label estimates, mark coverage gaps, and never present the management view as audited or filed figures.**

## 2. Recognition basis — when expenditure becomes cost

- **Accrual basis:** Ind AS 1.27–28 requires recognition of assets, liabilities, income and expenses when they meet the definitions/recognition criteria, not when cash moves. ICAI _Guidance Note on Accrual Basis of Accounting_ (GN(A) 6, 1988) §2.1–2.5: costs are recognised as incurred/earned, matched to revenues or the period; §4.4: costs of a future period are prepaid, costs of the period not yet paid are outstanding expenses.
- **Service period, not invoice or payment:**
  - Services are received **when performed by the supplier**, not when the receiver later uses them (Ind AS 38.69A), and payment in advance of receiving services supports a **prepayment asset** (Ind AS 38.70).
  - Employee cost accrues as **service is rendered** (Ind AS 19.11; Ind AS CF 4.43–4.47 — the salary example says the present obligation exists only once the employee's services are received, before that the contract is executory).
  - Received-but-not-invoiced amounts are **accruals**, not provisions (Ind AS 37.11(b)).
- **Practical rule:** four dates exist — order, delivery/acceptance (service period), invoice, payment. Recognise cost at the **service period**; the invoice is evidence, not the trigger. Accrue at month-end for services received without an invoice.
- **Recoverable taxes are not cost:** costs of purchase exclude taxes **recoverable** from tax authorities (Ind AS 2.11; Ind AS 16.16(a); Ind AS 38.27(a)); non-recoverable taxes and duties remain cost. GST input tax credit entitlement and blocked credits are governed by **CGST Act ss.16–17** (s.16 conditions: document, receipt of goods/services, tax paid to Government, return furnished; s.17(5) blocked credits; s.17(1)–(2) partial-use restriction). **No line-level eligibility conclusion may be drawn without documents** — eligibility is an evidence question, not a naming convention.
- **Cash is not cost:** paying a supplier settles a liability; it does not create a second cost. Report cash paid as a separate column/statement (the cash-flow view is part of the financial statements — Ind AS 1.10(d)).

## 3. Cost vs asset — what stays on the balance sheet

- An **asset** is a present economic resource controlled as a result of past events; a right to receive goods or services is such a resource (Ind AS CF 4.3–4.6). Recognition applies only when it provides useful information (Ind AS CF 5.6–5.7). If no asset exists, expenditure is expensed (Ind AS CF 5.10).
- **Advances and prepayments:** payment before services/goods are received is an asset until consumed (Ind AS 38.70; Ind AS 19.11(a) — excess paid over the accrued benefit is a prepaid expense). Once consumed, it becomes cost; if recovery is lost, it is written off.
- **Employee benefits:** liability and expense as service is rendered (Ind AS 19.11); accumulating paid absences as entitlement accrues (Ind AS 19.13–16); bonuses/profit share only when a present obligation (legal or constructive) and reliable estimate exist (Ind AS 19.19–23).
- **Costs to fulfil a customer contract:** an asset only if the costs are not within another standard and meet **all** of: relate directly to the contract, generate/enhance resources used for future performance, and are **expected to be recovered** (Ind AS 115.95). Otherwise expense when incurred (Ind AS 115.98); amortise consistent with transfer of the related services (Ind AS 115.99). Mobilisation/setup costs often fail this test (see Ind AS 115.B51).
- **Equipment and intangibles:** capitalise only what qualifies as PP&E (Ind AS 16.16–16.20) or an intangible (Ind AS 38.21–38.27; research expensed Ind AS 38.54; development criteria Ind AS 38.57); day-to-day servicing, training, start-up, admin and general overhead are expenses (Ind AS 16.12, 16.19; Ind AS 38.29, 38.68–69).

### IAS 2 scope caution (explicit)

- Ind AS 2/IAS 2 applies to **inventories**, which include materials or supplies to be consumed in rendering services (Ind AS 2.6(c)). It does **not** describe service-delivery project costs generally: costs to fulfil a customer contract that do not give rise to inventories are accounted for under **Ind AS 115** (Ind AS 2.8).
- Therefore **do not claim IAS 2 applies wholesale to a service firm's project costs.** Two of its mechanics remain useful management principles for internal costing, applied by policy:
  - recoverable taxes excluded from cost (2.11);
  - overhead absorption based on **normal capacity**, with unallocated overhead expensed (2.12–2.13), and abnormal waste expensed (2.16).
- Distinguish **standard scope** (where it applies) from **useful analogy** (how to allocate) in the report's methodology notes.

## 4. Direct project cost vs company overhead

- Ind AS 115.97 gives the direct-cost examples: direct labour, direct materials, allocations that relate directly to the contract (contract management/supervision, insurance, depreciation of tools/equipment used in fulfilling), costs explicitly chargeable, and subcontractor payments. General and administrative costs are expensed unless explicitly chargeable (Ind AS 115.98(a)); inefficiencies and non-proportionate costs do not depict progress (Ind AS 115.B19).
- Statutory reporting does **not** require project-level absorption of company overhead. Allocating overhead to projects is a **management accounting policy**; it needs a defined pool, driver, capacity basis and treatment of idle/unallocated amounts before it is reported.
- **Recommended composition** (consistent with ADR-0016 and ADR-0017):

| Layer                          | Content                                                                                                         | Where it appears                                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Project direct cost            | Allocated recorded payroll employer cost + recognized direct project expenses and supplier services or goods    | Summed into project totals                                                                   |
| Company overhead               | Deliberately classified shared operating expense                                                                | Inside company reconciliation; any approved project allocation remains separately identified |
| Unallocated recognised cost    | Recognized cost awaiting a reliable project or overhead classification, including unresolved payroll allocation | Outside project totals, inside company reconciliation                                        |
| Company recognised expenditure | Project direct cost + company overhead + unallocated recognized cost, each counted once                         | Reconciles to the books/control accounts                                                     |

- Guard rails: unallocated payroll is already part of company expenditure — it must not be re-added to project totals. Direct costs already allocated must not enter an overhead pool. The recommended model keeps deliberate overhead distinct from unknown allocation; any project overhead allocation needs an explicit management policy.

## 5. Contractual commitments are not cost

- A purchase order or contract for future goods/services is an **executory contract**: a combined right and obligation to exchange economic resources, unrecognised until a party performs (Ind AS CF 4.56–4.58). Ind AS 37.3: executory contracts are outside its scope unless onerous; Ind AS 37.67: many routine purchase orders can be cancelled without compensation and therefore create **no obligation**.
- Reporting definition: **Outstanding Supplier Commitment** = supplier order value not yet recognized as incurred cost, shown **separately** from Incurred Project Cost. Recognizing received goods or services consumes the corresponding commitment. A later invoice can replace an accrual; paying it settles a liability, not another cost (ADR-0017).
- Classify orders by **firm vs cancellable** and disclose the basis; do not treat genuine cancellable orders as certain future cost.
- Statutory precedent for disclosing commitments exists (contractual commitments for PPE acquisitions: Ind AS 16.74(c)), and onerous contracts are provided only when unavoidable costs exceed the expected benefits (Ind AS 37.66–69; impairments on dedicated assets are recognised first, 37.69). Future operating losses are never provisioned (Ind AS 37.63) — so a forecast loss does not become "cost" without an obligation or consumed service.

## 6. Recommended monthly management practice

1. **Cut-off and accrual capture.** At month-end, book accruals for services received without invoices (Ind AS 37.11(b)), age advances/prepayments, and mark every estimate (source, owner, basis, reversal expectation). Reverse/replace on actual evidence.
2. **Pending evidence register.** Track items whose recognition awaits: approved timesheets, service completion/GRN, supplier invoice matched to order, contract/change order, payment proof. Rule: no recognition without evidence or an explicitly labelled estimate; evidence gaps are disclosed, not silently omitted.
3. **Reconciliation.** Monthly: project ledger to GL/control accounts; payroll allocation to recorded payroll runs (ADR-0016); commitments rollforward (opening + new orders − recognized consumption − cancellations = closing); tax credits/balances to GST workings (eligibility evidenced per s.16/17); cash to payment records. Use consistent currency and tax bases. These follow the Conceptual Framework's verifiability and comparability qualities (Ind AS CF 2.24–2.31).
4. **Current estimated vs closed actuals.** The open month shows estimates separately from recorded costs. Payroll finalisation freezes employee allocation; it does not close supplier costs or the company's whole month. A separate financial close fixes monthly cost totals after accruals and source coverage are reviewed. Later corrections require explicit revisions. Reconstructed historical allocations stay labelled reconstructed and reviewed (ADR-0016). Compare equivalent elapsed periods and disclose differences in evidence completeness.
5. **Cost-to-date vs commitments vs budget.** Show each Project's cost-to-date, outstanding commitments, and approved cost budget on the same scope and currency basis. Client quotes or order values are commercial context, not cost budgets or recognized revenue. Flag likely losses for finance review (Ind AS 37.68, 115.101). Do not equate money spent with progress (Ind AS 115.B19; revenue progress methods, Ind AS 115.39–40, B14–B19).
6. **Presentation order.** Lead with **Incurred Project Cost**; show **Outstanding Supplier Commitment** and **cash paid** separately; keep taxes/assets (recoverable GST, prepayments/advances, capitalised items) out of cost entirely.

## 7. Coverage caveats from the current live profile (aggregate, 2026-10)

Disclose these limitations. Reliable reporting also requires complete source capture, project classification, and period controls:

- Several non-labour cost stores currently hold **no records**, so absence of rows is not absence of cost; state coverage per store.
- Some finalised payroll months lack the **original hours basis**; allocations must be labelled reconstructed (ADR-0016), not presented as the original.
- Most logged hours in the latest profiled month are **not linked to live projects** (about 2.9k hours profiled; only a minimal slice linked). This stays as "No project" disclosure — never redistributed to known projects.
- One pending purchase order is **ambiguous and linked to a draft client invoice**, and no vendor master records exist; commitment direction/amount is unverified. Resolve with documents, not table names.
- **Composition risk in estimated payroll cost (observed runtime, not a fix):** a test bonus input of 1,000 increased earnings by 1,000 **and** employer contributions by 1,000, i.e. employer cost by 2,000. Stored finalised slips contain no bonus amounts, so recorded history is unaffected. Any estimate produced by the same calculation must be reconciled to recorded payroll-slip employer cost (earnings vs employer contributions separately) before publication — estimates are not recorded cost (ADR-0016).

## 8. Caveats

- This note supports **management reporting only**; it does not create tax positions, does not certify statutory compliance, and does not override approved policies (ADR/glossary ownership stays with the parent).
- Tax eligibility (GST credits, non-recoverable taxes) is decided per item **with documents** under CGST Act ss.16–17; no company-specific conclusion is drawn here.
- IAS 2 is not applied wholesale to service project costs (see §3); Ind AS 115 governs contract-cost assets and the direct/indirect split.
- Estimates and allocations are only as good as the underlying evidence; where evidence is pending, disclose it.
